Chapter 13: Grover's search

Big idea. To find one marked item among \( N \), a classical computer checks about \( N/2 \) of them. Grover's algorithm needs about \( \sqrt{N} \) steps. Each step flips the sign of the marked item and then reflects every amplitude about the average, which pumps amplitude into the marked one. For four items, one step finds it with certainty.

The problem

You have a phone book with \( N \) names in random order and one phone number. Find the name that goes with it. There is no shortcut: on average you look at half the entries. Now suppose you have a box that, given a name, says yes or no. Classically you still need about \( N/2 \) calls.

Lov Grover showed in 1996 that a quantum computer can do it in about \( \tfrac{\pi}{4}\sqrt{N} \) calls. For a million entries that is about 800 instead of 500,000. It is not exponential speed-up like some algorithms, but it applies to almost any search problem, which makes it one of the most useful.

Setup

Use \( n \) qubits, so there are \( N = 2^n \) bit strings, one per item. Put H on every qubit to get the uniform state \( |s\rangle \), every item with amplitude \( \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{N}} \). The box is an oracle \( U_w \) that flips the sign of the marked item \( |w\rangle \) and leaves the rest alone. It is the phase-kickback box from Chapter 12, with the answer qubit already folded in:

\[ U_w|x\rangle = \begin{cases} -|x\rangle & x = w \\ |x\rangle & x \neq w \end{cases} \]

Flipping one sign does not change any probability (Chapter 6). We need a second step to turn the hidden sign into a visible probability.

The diffusion step: reflect about the average

The second step is a fixed gate \( D \) that does something simple to the list of amplitudes: compute their average \( m \), then replace every amplitude \( a \) by \( 2m - a \). That is a reflection about the average: an amplitude just above the mean ends up just below it, and one far below the mean ends up far above. In matrix form, \( D = 2|s\rangle\langle s| - I \), which you can read with Chapter 7: project onto the uniform state, double it, subtract the original.

Here is why that helps. After the oracle, the marked amplitude is negative and all the others are positive, so the average is slightly less than the others. Reflecting about it pushes the many positive ones down a little and throws the negative one far up above the mean. Amplitude moves from the crowd to the marked item.

Four items, by hand

Two qubits, \( N = 4 \), and suppose the marked item is \( w = 11 \). Follow the four amplitudes in order \( 00, 01, 10, 11 \).

Start. H on both qubits:

\[ |s\rangle = \tfrac{1}{2}\,(1,\ 1,\ 1,\ 1) \]

Oracle. Flip the sign of \( |11\rangle \):

\[ \tfrac{1}{2}\,(1,\ 1,\ 1,\ -1) \]

Diffusion. The average is \( m = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\tfrac{1 + 1 + 1 - 1}{4} = \tfrac{1}{4} \). Replace each \( a \) by \( 2m - a = \tfrac{1}{2} - a \):

\[ \big(\tfrac{1}{2} - \tfrac{1}{2},\ \tfrac{1}{2} - \tfrac{1}{2},\ \tfrac{1}{2} - \tfrac{1}{2},\ \tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{2}\big) = (0,\ 0,\ 0,\ 1) \]

The state is exactly \( |11\rangle \). Measure and you read the marked item with probability 1, after calling the oracle once. A classical search of four items needs up to three calls (or two if you can assume the last one by elimination).

The circuit for four items

Every piece is a gate you know. The oracle for \( w = 11 \) is a CZ (it flips the sign of \( |11\rangle \) only). To mark a different item, sandwich the CZ with X gates on the qubits that should be 0: for \( w = 01 \), put X on qubit 0 before and after. The diffusion gate \( D \) is "H on all, X on all, CZ, X on all, H on all". (The X-CZ-X part flips the sign of \( |00\rangle \); wrapping it in H gates turns "flip \( |00\rangle \)" into "flip \( |s\rangle \)", which is \( -D \), and the minus sign is a global phase.) 

q0: ─ H ─●─ H ─ X ─●─ X ─ H ─ M
         │         │
q1: ─ H ─●─ H ─ X ─●─ X ─ H ─ M
        oracle     diffusion

Bigger lists: the rotation picture

For larger \( N \), one step is not enough, and here is the cleanest way to see how many you need. Every state in the algorithm stays in the flat plane that contains both the marked item \( |w\rangle \) and the uniform state \( |s\rangle \). Draw that plane. The oracle is a reflection in one line, the diffusion is a reflection in another, and two reflections make a rotation. Each Grover step rotates the state towards \( |w\rangle \) by a fixed angle \( 2\theta \), where \( \sin\theta = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{N}} \).

You start at angle \( \theta \) from the "not marked" direction and want to reach \( 90^\circ \). So you need about \( \tfrac{\pi/2}{2\theta} \approx \tfrac{\pi}{4}\sqrt{N} \) steps (for large \( N \) the angle is small, and for small angles measured in radians \( \sin\theta \approx \theta \), so \( \theta \approx \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{N}} \)). Check with \( N = 4 \): \( \sin\theta = \tfrac{1}{2} \), so \( \theta = 30^\circ \), and one step of \( 60^\circ \) takes you from \( 30^\circ \) to exactly \( 90^\circ \). That is why four items need exactly one step.

Do not overshoot. Keep rotating past \( 90^\circ \) and the probability goes back down. Grover's algorithm must be stopped at the right step, unlike a classical search which only gets better the longer you look. If you do not know how many items are marked, there are versions that handle that.

What Grover is really for

The oracle can be any yes/no test you can build from gates: "does this key decrypt the message?", "does this colouring of the map use no clashing colours?", "does this schedule fit?". Any problem where you can check an answer quickly but have to search for it can be sped up by a square root. That covers a huge number of real problems, and it is also why some encryption key lengths are being doubled: a 128-bit key searched by Grover is only as strong as a 64-bit key.

Try it. The Quantum Lab challenge Grover search (2 qubits) asks for exactly the circuit above. Build it line by line: 
qc = QuantumCircuit(2)
qc.h(0); qc.h(1)          # uniform state
qc.cz(0, 1)               # oracle: mark |11>
qc.h(0); qc.h(1)          # diffusion begins
qc.x(0); qc.x(1)
qc.cz(0, 1)
qc.x(0); qc.x(1)
qc.h(0); qc.h(1)          # diffusion ends
The probabilities should show 100 percent for 11. Then change the oracle to mark 01 (add qc.x(0) before and after the first CZ) and watch the answer move.
Check yourself

Q1. After the oracle but before diffusion, what is the probability of measuring the marked item?

A1. Still \( \tfrac{1}{N} \). A sign flip changes no probability. The diffusion step is what makes it visible.

Q2. Roughly how many Grover steps for \( N = 1{,}000{,}000 \)?

A2. \( \tfrac{\pi}{4}\sqrt{10^6} \approx 0.785 \times 1000 \approx 785 \).

Q3. With \( N = 4 \), what happens if you run two Grover steps?

A3. The state rotates another \( 60^\circ \), to \( 150^\circ \). The probability of the marked item drops to \( \sin^2 150^\circ = \tfrac{1}{4} \), back to chance.
