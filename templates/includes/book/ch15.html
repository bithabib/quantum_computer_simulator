Chapter 15: Build it yourself in the Quantum Lab

Big idea. The Quantum Lab on this site runs real Python against a statevector simulator, the same maths as this book. This chapter is the command list, the challenges mapped to chapters, some projects to try on your own, and a glossary of every word the book introduced.

How the Lab works

Open the Quantum Lab, pick a challenge, and write Python in the box. Your code must build a circuit in a variable called qc. Press run. The page shows the measurement probabilities of every outcome, and the grader checks whether your state matches the target, phase included. If it does not, read the hints and try again. Nothing you do can break anything, so experiment.

The commands

qc.x(q), qc.y(q), qc.z(q)Pauli gates on qubit \( q \)10
qc.h(q)Hadamard: \( |0\rangle \to |+\rangle \)10
qc.s(q), qc.sdg(q)Quarter turn of phase, and its inverse10
qc.t(q), qc.tdg(q)Eighth turn of phase, and its inverse10
qc.rx(theta, q), qc.ry(theta, q), qc.rz(theta, q)Rotation by angle theta (radians) about an axis10
qc.p(lam, q)Phase gate \( P(\lambda) \): multiply the \( |1\rangle \) amplitude by \( e^{i\lambda} \)10
qc.cx(c, t)CNOT with control \( c \), target \( t \)11
qc.cz(c, t)Controlled-Z: flip the sign of \( |11\rangle \)11, 13
qc.cp(lam, c, t)Controlled phase: multiply the \( |11\rangle \) amplitude by \( e^{i\lambda} \)11
qc.swap(a, b)Exchange two qubits11
qc.ccx(c1, c2, t)Toffoli: flip \( t \) if both controls are 111
qc.statevector()The list of amplitudes, in order \( 00\ldots0 \) to \( 11\ldots1 \)7
qc.probabilities()The list of \( |\text{amplitude}|^2 \)6
qc.draw()Print the circuit12

Qubit numbering starts at 0, and qubit 0 is the left digit of each outcome label (Chapter 11). Angles are in radians: use 3.14159 for \( \pi \), or import math and math.pi.

Projects with no grader

Any challenge box will run any circuit of the right size; you can ignore the grader and just read the probabilities.

  1. Set a chance. Make a one-qubit state that reads 1 exactly 25 percent of the time. (Chapter 10: \( R_y(\theta) \) with \( \sin^2\tfrac{\theta}{2} = \tfrac{1}{4} \), so \( \theta = \tfrac{\pi}{3} \).)
  2. See a phase. Build \( |+\rangle \), apply qc.p(lam, 0) for several values of lam, then apply H. Plot the probability of 1 against lam. You should get \( \sin^2\tfrac{\lambda}{2} \): interference turning a phase into a chance.
  3. All four Bell states. Start from the Bell pair and add X, or Z, or Z then X, on qubit 1. Check the amplitudes match Chapter 11's list.
  4. Deutsch for all four functions. Chapter 12 gives the code for one. Write the other three and confirm qubit 0 reads 0, 0, 1, 1.
  5. Grover with a different mark. Mark 00, 01 and 10 in turn (Chapter 13). Then try three qubits, \( N = 8 \): mark \( |111\rangle \) with a controlled-controlled-Z built as qc.h(2); qc.ccx(0, 1, 2); qc.h(2). The diffusion step is the same pattern as before on all three qubits: H on all, X on all, that same controlled-controlled-Z, X on all, H on all. One step should give about 78 percent for 111 and two steps about 95 percent.
  6. Teleportation. Build Chapter 14's circuit on three qubits. The Lab has no measurement-controlled gates, so instead apply qc.cx(1, 2) and qc.cz(0, 2) at the end; that is the same fix applied "quantumly". Start with any one-qubit state on qubit 0 (for example qc.ry(1.0, 0)) and check that the probabilities of qubit 2 match it.

Glossary

Amplitude
A complex number attached to an outcome. Its length squared is the probability. (Ch. 6)
Basis
A set of arrows that can build every other arrow. \( \{|0\rangle, |1\rangle\} \) and \( \{|+\rangle, |-\rangle\} \) are both bases of a qubit. (Ch. 2)
Bell pair
The entangled state \( \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|00\rangle + |11\rangle) \). (Ch. 11)
Bloch sphere
A ball whose surface shows every one-qubit state. (Ch. 8)
Born rule
Probability equals amplitude length squared. (Ch. 6)
Bra
\( \langle\psi| \): the transposed, conjugated row version of a ket. (Ch. 7)
CNOT
Controlled NOT: flip the target if the control is 1. (Ch. 11)
Collapse
After measurement, the state becomes the outcome you saw. (Ch. 9)
Complex conjugate
\( z^* \): flip the sign of the imaginary part. \( z z^* = |z|^2 \). (Ch. 5)
Complex number
\( a + bi \) with \( i^2 = -1 \); an arrow with a length and an angle. (Ch. 5)
Dagger
\( \dagger \): transpose and conjugate. For a gate, the dagger is the inverse. (Ch. 7)
Decoherence
Loss of superposition through contact with the outside world. (Ch. 14)
Derivative
Rate of change; the slope of a graph. (Ch. 4)
Diffusion
Grover's reflect-about-the-average step. (Ch. 13)
Dot product
Multiply matching entries and add. Measures how much two arrows agree. (Ch. 2)
Eigenvector, eigenvalue
An arrow a matrix only stretches, and the stretch factor. Measurement outcomes are eigenvectors. (Ch. 3, 9)
Entanglement
A multi-qubit state that cannot be split into one state per qubit. (Ch. 11)
Gate
A unitary matrix applied to qubits. (Ch. 10)
Global phase
A factor \( e^{i\theta} \) on the whole state; physically meaningless. (Ch. 8)
Hadamard (H)
The gate that turns \( |0\rangle \) into \( |+\rangle \) and back. (Ch. 10)
Hilbert space
Arrows with complex entries and an inner product. Where states live. (Ch. 5)
Inner product
\( \langle\phi|\psi\rangle \): the dot product with conjugation on the left. (Ch. 7)
Interference
Amplitudes for different paths adding or cancelling before squaring. (Ch. 6)
Ket
\( |\psi\rangle \): a column vector written in physicists' notation. (Ch. 2, 7)
Matrix
A grid of numbers that turns one arrow into another. (Ch. 3)
Measurement
Asking a qubit a question and getting a random answer with the Born rule's probabilities. (Ch. 9)
No-cloning
No gate can copy an unknown quantum state. (Ch. 9)
Normalise
Divide an arrow by its length so it has length 1. (Ch. 2)
Oracle
A gate that marks the answer to a yes/no question, usually by a sign flip. (Ch. 12, 13)
Orthogonal, orthonormal
At right angles; at right angles and of length 1. (Ch. 2)
Phase
The angle of a complex number. Relative phase between amplitudes is physical. (Ch. 5, 8)
Phase kickback
Moving a function's answer into the phase of the input qubit. (Ch. 12)
Qubit
A two-level quantum system; a unit arrow \( \alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle \). (Ch. 8)
Superposition
A state that is a mix of basis states. (Ch. 8)
Tensor product
\( \otimes \): gluing two systems into one by multiplying out the brackets. (Ch. 11)
Unitary
A matrix with \( U^\dagger U = I \). Keeps lengths. Every gate is one. (Ch. 3, 7)
Vector
An arrow; a list of numbers. (Ch. 2)
